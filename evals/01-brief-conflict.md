# Eval 01 — Brief Conflict

## Purpose

Test whether the agent distinguishes factual requirements from subjective preference language when stronger visual preference evidence exists.

## Synthetic project

A regional engineering contractor needs a new corporate website.

### Locked facts

- Services: heating, ventilation, cooling, and technical station construction.
- References are the primary proof mechanism.
- No fabricated metrics, certifications, customers, or testimonials.
- The company has a red, black, and white identity.

### Written aesthetic direction

The stakeholder describes the desired site as calm, factual, technical, stable, and "not salesy." They say red should be used as an accent.

### Demonstrated preference evidence

The stakeholder gives the highest ratings to examples with:

- large documentary industrial photography;
- strong red brand presence;
- immediately visible service categories;
- prominent but conventional project/contact actions;
- clear service cards and icons;
- dark/light section contrast;
- bold brand geometry.

They reject a highly restrained editorial concept with very little red, sparse calls to action, and architecture-studio composition.

## Task

Propose the homepage visual direction and section architecture. Explain only the decisions that materially affect the design. Do not invent business facts.

## Failure patterns

- literalizing "not salesy" into weak navigation or hidden actions;
- interpreting "technical" as monospace labels, grids, fake diagrams, or telemetry;
- using red so sparingly that the existing brand loses presence;
- replacing the demonstrated category preference with an architecture/editorial aesthetic;
- fabricating metrics or certifications to create proof;
- copying the preferred references literally instead of extracting their underlying characteristics.

## Strong behavior

The agent preserves factual constraints while treating demonstrated selections as stronger evidence for subjective visual decisions. It should propose a highly legible, image-led engineering-company experience with meaningful brand presence and conventional usability, then create originality through company-specific geometry, imagery, content, and composition rather than changing the visual category.

## Scoring

Score 1–5 for factual grounding, preference inference, category fit, brand specificity, composition, usability, and restraint. Record evidence for every score.
